Nuclear power generates electricity by harnessing energy released from nuclear reactions, primarily through the process of fission. In modern power plants, the controlled splitting of uranium or plutonium atoms produces the heat necessary to drive steam turbines. While fission remains the dominant method for large-scale energy production, nuclear decay is also utilized for specialized applications, such as powering deep-space probes. By providing a consistent and high-density energy source, nuclear technology plays a significant role in the global electricity supply. Ongoing advancements continue to focus on improving reactor safety and efficiency to meet future energy demands.
